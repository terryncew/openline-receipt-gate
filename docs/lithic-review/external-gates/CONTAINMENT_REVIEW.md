## Operational stop criteria

Before a case, the future caller must have its exact approved body, one remaining request allowance, the expected card/program and a reviewed probe mode. No next POST until the previous attempt has a reconciled final provider authorization outcome and callback activity has been accounted for. SDK/manual retry is forbidden after ambiguity. A simulation201 or local signed decline alone is not a final provider result.

Stop on provider APPROVED or partial approval, an unknown/ambiguous final result, extra simulator POST or unexpected token/card/amount/currency/operation, unresolved delivery after the finite horizon, invalid HMAC, unsupported/drifting payload, altered signing pin/state, containment discrepancy or unapproved cost. A provider result hidden by a native card decline does not count as evidence of fail-closed ASA behavior.

The abort procedure first **latches the simulation caller off** so no new attempt can be sent. Preserve the in-flight transaction IDs and private evidence. Use only separately approved read-only reconciliation. For any subsequent authenticated callback, the preplanned safe responder mode should be a valid decline with no authority; do not retain a fault mode or stop/remove the endpoint without understanding its fallback consequences. Emergency issuer card pause or endpoint changes must be separately preapproved and performed by the named authorized operator. Never assume teardown cancels a committed provider approval.

Native card caps/merchant rules may be independent containment controls when their semantics and ordering are established, but a PAUSED/deny-all card can suppress ASA and make the fault trial nondiscriminating. Keep the safety-control record and the authorization evidence separate. Do not claim to measure provider fallback through a short-circuiting issuer decline.
