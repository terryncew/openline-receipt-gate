# Alternatives and acceptance observables: one-card $1 ASA experiment

Reviewed read-only on 2026-10-11 at receipt-gate checkout `8a537d4ff598541bb21cc564b3220ec97ef09fb3`. This is an assessment of the implemented PR #108 preflight and candidate owner experiment. No provider API, external service, enrollment, card, contact, deployment, or repository change was made. The only new file is this scratch assessment.

## Decision

The current evidence supports offline readiness for owner review, not an external sandbox result or a uniquely necessary OpenLine architecture. Ordinary card controls solve a bounded spending experiment. A conventional stateful authorization gate can reproduce the implemented revocation, holder binding, one-use intent, durable retries, and signed decisions. The defensible OpenLine hypothesis is that its existing portable owner-authority format can be enforced by a separately operated receiver with an independently authenticated owner pin and can replace a worker on the same card. That is an integration and trust-boundary claim, not a novel cryptographic capability.

Choose ordinary card controls if the only requirement is a $1 cap, merchant restriction, or stopping the card. Choose the OpenLine experiment only if the owner wants evidence about portable delegation and revocation across the owner/worker/receiver boundary.

## What the actual owner proof contains

The Wallet mandate has exactly `mandate_id`, `subject_id`, `subject_public_key`, `scopes`, `expires_at`, and predecessor identity. A grant is signed by a root-certified epoch key; revocation and the exported bundle are signed by the owner root. The library scope is a string such as `lithic:authorize`. There is no amount, card, merchant, operation-context, or budget field in the owner mandate.

The adapter independently requires an open `VIRTUAL` card and exactly 100 USD cents in both structured amounts, unit conversion, a cardholder purchase authorization, no cash or fees, and consistent legacy fields. The worker separately signs the card/payment details, receiver ID, mandate/subject identities, and Wallet presentation hash. Therefore the evidence is **owner delegation of a scope + receiver $1 policy + worker-signed payment intent**. It does not establish that the owner cryptographically approved the complete specific payment terms.

Only one pending authorization exists per card. It lasts at most 60 seconds and admits the next matching authenticated ASA purchase. Two purchases with identical selected terms are indistinguishable before the Lithic transaction token exists; either may consume the pending intent first. No unique merchant order, goods, settlement, recurring charge, clearing, or general-purpose spend-budget authorization is demonstrated.

Sources: `gateway/receiver-package/openline_receiver/openline_wallet/wallet.py:165,669,733,762`; `openline_receiver/lithic_adapter.py:58,112,262,346,409,446`; `LITHIC_CONTRACT.md`.

## Fair comparison

| Ordinary card state, spend limits, merchant/conditional rules | Pause/reopen the card; cap amounts; restrict merchant/currency/initiator; provider rules can test exact amount equality. A lifetime allowance can limit total approved authorizations. | Control is expressed through the issuer/program's authenticated control plane. This alone does not demonstrate portable owner-signed worker delegation enforced by an independent receiver. Availability to a particular sandbox/program remains untested. |
| Basic stateless signed JWT/JWS | Authentic grant, expiry and static action/subject/audience restrictions, with a suitably pinned issuer key. Holder proof can be added. | A token by itself does not persist revocation state, atomically consume permission once, reject an admitted earlier authority head, or preserve a committed decision after crash. A bearer token also does not prove possession of the mandated worker key. Comparing only this weak baseline would overstate OpenLine's benefit. |

This can implement the same enforced sequence. | Requires the same authenticated trust bootstrap, delivered revocation, clock/freshness rules, protected persistent state and trusted enforcement operator. These are published SDK semantics, not a claim that this owner's program has every feature enabled or that rule precedence with ASA was verified.

## Proposed owner hypothesis and pass criteria

**Hypothesis:** With one sandbox VIRTUAL card left open, unchanged provider spending/merchant controls and sufficient remaining provider allowance, an independently pinned receiver accepts worker A's valid Wallet authority and one $1 matching intent; an owner-signed revocation delivered and admitted before a new matching purchase invalidates an already pending A intent durably; stale A authority cannot restore it; a separately owner-granted worker B can authorize the same $1 operation on the same card after a fresh presentation and intent. An observer using a receiver key authenticated and pinned before any response can verify the resulting decision receipts.

Every following observable is necessary to attribute success to portable authority enforcement rather than ordinary spending controls:

1. Record the actual card/account-to-owner relationship and provenance of the independently authenticated owner-root pin. Record the receiver public-key fingerprint and provenance before the first ASA response. Separate owner, A, B and receiver signing keys; workers must not control receiver administrative operations. A key embedded in a bundle/receipt is not evidence of trusted enrollment.
2. Snapshot/redact provider card state, spend-limit value and duration, relevant merchant/authorization rules, and remaining allowance at each stage. Keep them unchanged. The card remains `OPEN`; the ASA result string `CARD_PAUSED` does not imply a provider card-state mutation. Show that no pause, closure, issuer rule, empty provider budget or cap exhaustion caused the revocation-stage refusal.
3. Admit owner-signed A grant and verify that card binding alone declines. A then obtains a fresh receiver challenge, signs a presentation and normalized full payment intent, and creates pending permission. A fresh, authenticated $1 ASA transaction is approved with a committed signed receipt. Preserve the authenticated request fingerprint and transaction token, without exporting credentials.
4. Create another valid, **unconsumed and unexpired** A pending intent before revocation. Record its successful preauthorization and time/expiry. Then the owner signs revoke, exports the newer bundle, and the receiver admits it successfully. Record old/new authority heads, admission completion and ordering. The receiver learns revocation only at admission; a revoke retained in the owner Wallet is not sufficient.
5. Restart/reopen the receiver with the original receiver-key pin and retained durable state. While the pending intent would otherwise still be valid, send a **new transaction token** with the same matching $1 payment terms. Require decline, unchanged provider controls/remaining allowance, a committed signed receipt and refusal to admit older signed bundles or freshly exported old heads. Test stale presentations/old worker A authorization remains denied. An identical retry of the original committed approval is expected to return that approval and is not a fresh revocation test.
6. Owner grants B a new `lithic:authorize` mandate/key, exports/admit the new bundle, and the operator binds the **same card** to B. No cap reset/card reopening is used to make this succeed. B supplies a new challenge/presentation/intent, then a new matching $1 ASA transaction approves, including after restart. Rebinding itself clears prior pending authority, so B success must not substitute for the pre-rebind revocation test.
7. Retrieve A approval, new post-revocation decline and B approval receipts through the scoped read-only credential. Independently check canonical hash and Ed25519 signature against the prior receiver pin, result, card/payment terms, transaction token/content fingerprint, owner/worker identities and authority proof where present. Verify the signed owner grant/revoke/successor bundle chains against the prior owner pin separately. Record complete response timings; offline timings do not establish deployed ASA round-trip behavior.
8. Include an intent mismatch control (such as another merchant with the same $1 amount) that declines without consuming the intended permission, and consumption/replay/stale-head checks. Keep matching attempts controlled: the first-match contract cannot distinguish identical competing purchases.

Do not use a 100-cent `FOREVER` provider limit as the causal test of revocation: it can be exhausted by A's first approval and also block B's approval. A `TRANSACTION` 100-cent limit is a useful provider safety control but does not limit aggregate spend or require exactly $1; the receiver supplies the exact-value restriction. Any total sandbox experiment ceiling must allow the two intended $1 approvals while retaining safety constraints. Authorization spend is not settlement; declines also remain attempts.

## What the existing evidence does and does not establish

`LITHIC_PREFLIGHT.md` and `LITHIC_CONTRACT.md` explicitly limit readiness to offline preflight/owner review, synthetic identifiers and localhost. `test_lithic_preflight.py:140` supplies the required laboratory approval -> unconsumed pending A -> admitted owner revoke -> restart/new-token decline -> new B grant/same-card approval sequence. Other existing tests cover stale heads, races, process/crash behavior and corruption. I read these sources and preserved evidence; I did not rerun suites or claim a new independently operated deployment.

`verify_pilot_receipts.py` independently checks receipt hash/signature and the three-result sequence. It **does not** independently verify the owner bundles or worker intent/signature bindings. Receiving a valid signature proves the pinned receiver signed that payload; it does not prove the host ran the claimed code or that no operator changed policy. The public preflight bundles can support a separate authority-chain verification, but that is distinct from this standalone receipt verifier.

Revocation admission deletes A's pending row. Consequently the subsequent signed decline ordinarily contains `NO_PENDING_AUTHORIZATION` and `authority_proof: null`, rather than a proof naming the admitted revocation head. The separate pending/expiry/admission/provider-control observables above are required to establish causal attribution. Neither receipt signatures nor result ordering alone prove that revocation caused refusal.

Both OpenLine and the equivalent stateful construction trust authenticated out-of-band delivery of pins, card/account ownership mapping, the receiver operator and host, secure signing key/state storage, clock/freshness policy, and successful delivery/admission of revocations. A host administrator can rewrite coherent state or restore an older complete database; anti-rollback across such restoration is unsupported. There is no claimed protection against a malicious administrator, immediate effect of undelivered revocation, automatic root succession, or production disaster recovery.

## Material blockers and scope boundaries

There is no additional offline alternatives-research blocker. This assessment can complete now. Actual sandbox connectivity/enrollment, public TLS/ASA transport, real program signing secret and verified card/account mapping are external pilot prerequisites explicitly left outside the completed preflight. They require owner-approved operator work, not further speculative research.

If the desired hypothesis requires **owner-signed exact $1/card/merchant terms**, the existing mandate format does not meet it; that is a material capability gap and needs a separate design/code change or a narrower claim accepted by the owner. If the desired hypothesis requires **cryptographic proof that a decline was caused by a particular admitted revocation**, existing receipt/verifier scope does not meet it alone; retain the causal observations or separately extend evidence design. If the goal only requires a capped authorization and a way to stop spend, ordinary card controls are the simpler sufficient choice.

Passing the proposed sequence supports this bounded integration's portable owner-authority enforcement on a trusted receiver. It does not distinguish OpenLine from a correctly built equivalent owner-signed stateful gate, establish cross-vendor interoperability, or show that OpenLine is uniquely necessary.
